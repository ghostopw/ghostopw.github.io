Special Educational Supplement

A Practical Guide to Starting in Artificial Intelligence

Where to begin, where to go next, and how to train: the whole territory, charted

The path I walked and the map I wish I'd had, by G. Guimarães

⁂

I didn't start with any special advantage. In 2023, between engineering classes, I took work at Outlier AI training and evaluating large language models, and I discovered that the fastest way into AI is to work near it daily, even at the edges. Nobody sends you an invitation. You simply begin.

From there, the path was unglamorous and effective: free courses (CS50's Introduction to AI, Google's AI Essentials, Python crash courses), small projects published openly on GitHub, a hackathon won with an end-to-end pipeline, a dataset published with a DOI and, in time, AI engineering work in public security and a research scholarship. Every step was small. The sum was not.

What follows is the map I wish someone had handed me on day one: the modern AI ecosystem, from mathematical foundations to applied products, ordered as a curriculum a beginner can actually follow, one stage at a time.

Read it top to bottom if you are just beginning; jump straight to your stage if you are already on the road. And if you get lost along the way, write to me. I answer.

The Curriculum

Stage I
The Foundations

Mathematics, statistics, and computer science: the base on which everything above is built. Skip this layer and everything on top of it wobbles.

Linear AlgebraCalculusProbability & StatisticsPythonAlgorithms & Data StructuresOptimisation

How to train: choose Python, solve small problems daily rather than weekly, and implement the mathematics you study. A gradient you have coded yourself is worth ten you have only read about.

Stage II
Machine Learning

The core craft: teaching machines to learn from data and, just as important, learning to tell when a model is lying to you.

Supervised LearningUnsupervised LearningReinforcementGenerative ModelsEmbeddingsFeature EngineeringModel Selection & Evaluation

How to train: take a public dataset and carry a prediction end to end (cleaning, features, model, honest evaluation). Then repeat with messier data. Kaggle and hackathons are the sparring grounds of this stage.

Stage III
Deep Learning

Artificial neural networks and the architectures behind modern AI, from the humble perceptron to the Transformers that power today's large language models.

Neural NetworksCNNsRNNs / LSTMsTransformersLarge Language ModelsPyTorch

How to train: build one small network from scratch, once; it is a rite of passage. Then use PyTorch for everything else: fine-tune something modest, break it, and understand precisely why it broke.

Stage IV
AI Engineering

Where models meet reality: building, orchestrating, and operating AI systems that survive contact with real users. This layer is where my own daily work lives.

Prompt EngineeringRAG & Semantic SearchAI AgentsFine-tuning & AdaptationEvaluation & GuardrailsLLMOps / MLOpsObservability

How to train: build a RAG chatbot over documents you genuinely care about, and add evaluation before you add features. Ship it to one real user and listen to what they say.

Stage V
Applied AI

The outermost layer: solutions that produce value in the real world, such as automation, assistants, decision support, and AI products that people actually use.

Process AutomationIntelligent AssistantsAnalysis & Decision-MakingAI Products & ServicesCustomer ExperienceInnovation & New Models

How to train: find a repetitive problem inside a real business (there are thousands), automate it, and measure the hours saved. Impact is the final examination.

Ongoing
Enabling Infrastructure

None of the layers stands alone. Beneath the whole ecosystem lies the infrastructure that carries it. Learn it gradually, exactly as your projects demand it.

Cloud & Multi-cloudGPUs / TPUsStorage & DatabasesAPIs & ServicesQueues & StreamingNetworks & SecurityCI/CD & DevOps

How to train: deploy every project you build, however small. A model in a notebook is a rumour; a model behind an API is a fact.

Six Principles for the Journey

Foundation

Mathematical and computational knowledge. The deeper the roots, the taller the tower you may build upon them.

Capability

The ability to build and operate AI systems, earned by building, never by watching others build.

Data

Quality, management, and governance. A model is only as honest as the data it was raised on.

Trust

Security, ethics, privacy, and compliance. Power without care causes real harm; build responsibly.

Scalability

Infrastructure, cost, and performance. What works for one must be made to work for many.

Impact

Efficiency and transformation. The true measure of the craft is what it changes in the world.
